Appendix H2 The half point and rounding

Bek Marat   M2

Part a Feasibility and cost

Take y = (0.500,0.500,0.500). Each variable is between 0 and 1. Each covering row has left side 0.500 + 0.500 = 1.000 so all three rows hold with equality.

The cost is 8 x 0.500 + 7 x 0.500 + 7 x 0.500 = 4.000 + 3.500 + 3.500 = 11.000.

The dual vector (4,3,4) is nonnegative and A^T pi = (8,7,7) = c. Its lower bound is 4 + 3 + 4 = 11.000. The feasible half point reaches this bound so zLP = 11.000.

Part b Rounding up and down

Rounding | Point | Feasibility | Cost

Up | (1,1,1) | All row left sides are 2 | 22.000

Down | (0,0,0) | All three rows fail by 1 | 0.000

Rounding up is feasible but costly. Rounding down is infeasible even though its objective is zero. Its cost cannot be used as the value of a feasible solution.

Part c The integer optimum and relative LP gap

H1 gives z* = 14.000 at (0,1,1).

(z* - zLP) / z* = (14 - 11) / 14 = 3 / 14 = 0.214285714 = 21.429 percent.

Every feasible integer point costs at least 14.000 while the LP has cost 11.000. Therefore no integral point can be an optimal solution of this LP. The relative gap measures the weakness of the relaxation when the integer optimum is known.
